10 things to do with Grandma and Grandad at Christmas

Visiting grandparents over Christmas? Here are 10 easy activities for grandchildren and grandparents to do together, for ages 6 to 96. Plus 6 free "Ask Grandma" question cards to print.

10 ideas

  1. Ask Grandma. Take turns asking questions about Christmas long ago. Print the free cards below.
  2. Then or now? Name something Christmassy: black and white telly, video calls, crackers. Was it then, now, or both?
  3. Teach me, I'll teach you. Grandad teaches a card game like Snap, and the grandchild teaches him a selfie or an emoji.
  4. Bake together. Chocolate cornflake nests need no oven: grandparents stir, children spoon and decorate.
  5. A paper chain of love. On each strip, write something you love about each other, then loop them together for the tree.
  6. A memory jar. Write a Christmas memory on a slip of paper each visit, and read them all out next year.
  7. Picture bingo. Pictures and big words, so non-readers and anyone with poor eyesight can join in.
  8. Say it, draw it. One describes a Christmas thing without saying it, the other draws it.
  9. Carols and old songs. Ask which carol they sang at school, and sing it together. Music often brings back the best stories.
  10. Make a keepsake. A photo, a handprint each and "Our Christmas" written underneath. Keep it with the decorations.

If Grandma or Grandad has memory problems

  • Follow their lead: if a question doesn't spark anything, move on. There are no wrong answers.
  • Pictures, music and making things together often work better than questions.
  • Keep it short, and stop while it's still fun.
  • Explain gently to children beforehand that Grandma might repeat things or forget names.
