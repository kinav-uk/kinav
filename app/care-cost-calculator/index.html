When does the council help pay for care?

The council looks at the savings and income of the person who needs care, not yours.

  • Over £23,250: they usually pay the full cost of their care.
  • £14,250 to £23,250: they pay from their income, plus £1 a week for every £250 of savings over £14,250. The council pays the rest.
  • Under £14,250: savings are ignored, and they pay from their income only.

Is the house counted?

  • Their home is never counted for care at home.
  • For a care home, it is counted, but not for the first 12 weeks, and not at all if a partner, or a relative who is 60 or over or disabled, still lives there.
  • If it does count, ask the council for a deferred payment agreement, so it doesn't have to be sold straight away.

Needs come first

The council's free needs assessment decides whether they qualify for care. Anyone can ask for one, whatever their savings. A financial assessment then works out who pays. Citizens Advice (0800 144 8848) can help with the financial assessment or a charging decision.

These are estimates and general information about England's rules, not financial advice. Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland have different limits.
