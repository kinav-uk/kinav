Choosing a nursing home

Shortlist, visit and ask the right questions.

Tick off each step as you go in the free Kinav app.

Open this checklist

Step by step

  1. Ask the council for a free needs assessment
    It confirms what level of care is needed. Anyone can ask, whatever their savings.
  2. Work out how it will be paid for
    Use the Care costs tool. If savings are above £23,250 they will usually pay their own fees at first. Ask the council for a financial assessment either way.
  3. If their home is the main asset
    The value of their home is ignored for the first 12 weeks in a care home, and always if a partner or certain relatives still live there. Ask the council about a deferred payment agreement, which lets fees be paid from the home's value later, so it doesn't have to be sold straight away. The Society of Later Life Advisers can find an accredited adviser.
  4. Check who can manage their money
    To deal with their bank, pension or the house, you'll need a property and financial affairs Lasting Power of Attorney. It can only be made while they can still decide. If it's too late, you can apply to the Court of Protection to become their deputy, which takes months. For benefits only, the DWP can make you their appointee. See the No Lasting Power of Attorney kit.
  5. Shortlist 3 to 5 homes
    Use Find care, filter to nursing homes and dementia care if needed, and read each home's latest CQC report.
  6. Phone each home
    Say: "I'm looking for a nursing place for my mum. Do you have a vacancy, what is the weekly fee, and what does it include?" Ask whether they keep residents if their savings run out and the council starts paying.
  7. Visit at least twice
    Go once at a mealtime. Look for staff talking with residents, call bells answered quickly, residents doing things, and a clean, fresh home.
  8. Ask the manager
    How many nurses are on each shift? How much do you use agency staff? How do you support someone who becomes distressed? How often does a GP visit? Can residents stay here to the end of their life?
  9. Ask about NHS help with nursing costs
    In a nursing home, the NHS pays a weekly contribution towards nursing care, called NHS-funded Nursing Care, straight to the home. Check the fee quoted shows it. Ask about an NHS Continuing Healthcare check too: if they qualify, the NHS pays the full cost.
  10. Read the contract before signing
    Use the Before you sign kit.
  11. Plan the move
    Bring familiar things and photos, label clothes, and tell the GP, pharmacy, bank, pension providers and the DWP about the new address. Ask whether they will stay with their GP or move to the home's GP. Check whether Attendance Allowance or PIP stops after 28 days if the council helps pay the fees.

Sources: Care Quality Commission (CQC) · Age UK: care and support. Last reviewed 24 October 2026.
