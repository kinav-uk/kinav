NHS Continuing Healthcare

If their needs are mainly health needs, the NHS pays for all their care, at home or in a care home. England.

Step by step

  1. Know what it is
    Care fully funded by the NHS for people whose main need is a health need. It isn't means-tested. It depends on how complex, intense or unpredictable their needs are, not on a diagnosis.
  2. Ask for the checklist
    Ask a nurse, social worker or the GP to complete the NHS Continuing Healthcare checklist. In hospital, ask before they're discharged. It's a short screening step.
  3. The full assessment
    If the checklist is positive, a team of professionals assesses them using the Decision Support Tool, which looks at 12 areas such as breathing, eating, mobility, behaviour and medicines. The NHS aims to decide within 28 days.
  4. Take part and bring evidence
    You can be there. Bring a diary of their needs over a few weeks, day and night, and any care records. Say if you think something has been missed.
  5. If time is short
    If they're near the end of life and getting worse quickly, a doctor or nurse can use the fast-track route so care can start within days.
  6. Get copies
    Ask for copies of the checklist, the Decision Support Tool and the decision letter.
  7. If it's refused
    Write to the Integrated Care Board (ICB) within 6 months of the decision to ask for a review. If you're still unhappy, ask NHS England for an independent review.
  8. If it's not Continuing Healthcare
    In a nursing home, they may get NHS-funded Nursing Care, a weekly NHS payment towards nursing costs. Ask about it.
  9. Reviews
    If they get it, it's reviewed after 3 months and then at least once a year.
  10. Get free help
    Age UK 0800 678 1602. Citizens Advice Adviceline (England) 0800 144 8848.

Sources: NHS: NHS continuing healthcare · Age UK: care and support. Last reviewed 24 October 2026.
