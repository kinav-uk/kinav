Fall alarms and home cameras

Get help quickly after a fall, and use cameras with care.

Tick off each step as you go in the free Kinav app.

Open this checklist

Step by step

  1. Ask the council about a personal alarm
    Most councils run a telecare or lifeline service: a button worn on the wrist or neck that calls a 24-hour team. Ask during the needs assessment or OT visit. There is usually a weekly charge.
  2. Choose the right type of alarm
    A button they press; an automatic fall detector that calls for help even if they can't press it; a GPS alarm if they go out alone; or a smartwatch with fall detection. If they might forget to press a button, choose automatic fall detection.
  3. Pick who responds
    Give the alarm service 2 or 3 people who live nearby and can get there quickly. Some services offer their own responders if family is far away.
  4. Fit a key safe
    A key safe by the front door lets responders and ambulance crews get in without breaking the door. Choose a police-approved model.
  5. Make sure it's worn
    Most fall alarms are waterproof, so they can be worn in the shower, where many falls happen. Test it once a month.
  6. Consider sensors instead of cameras
    Bed, chair and door sensors, or activity sensors that learn their routine, can alert you to a fall or a night-time wander without filming them.
  7. If you want cameras, talk to them first
    If they can make the decision, they must agree. If they can't, any camera must be in their best interests and the least intrusive option. Involve family and anyone with a health and welfare Power of Attorney.
  8. Keep cameras out of private rooms
    Don't put cameras in bedrooms or bathrooms. Avoid recording sound unless there's a clear reason, as it's more intrusive.
  9. Tell carers about any cameras
    Let care workers and the agency know where cameras are and why. Recording people without telling them damages trust.
  10. Keep the cameras private and secure
    Change the default password and turn on two-step login. If a camera films beyond their property, such as the street or a neighbour's garden, data protection law applies, so point cameras inside the home.
