No Lasting Power of Attorney?

When they can no longer decide: deputyship, appointees and best-interests decisions. England and Wales.

Tick off each step as you go in the free Kinav app.

Open this checklist

Step by step

  1. Check for an older power of attorney
    An Enduring Power of Attorney made before October 2007 can still be used, but it must be registered with the Office of the Public Guardian once they can't manage. You can ask the Office of the Public Guardian to search its registers, free, to check for an LPA, EPA or deputy.
  2. Check whether they could still make an LPA
    Capacity depends on the decision and can vary from day to day. If they might still understand, ask the GP or a solicitor whether they could make one now. It's quicker and cheaper than the court route.
  3. Only benefits and pension? Become their appointee
    If their income is just State Pension and benefits, call the helpline for the benefit and ask to become their appointee. It's free. The DWP visits them and interviews you first. It doesn't cover bank savings or property.
  4. Savings, pensions or property? Apply to be their deputy
    A property and financial affairs deputy is appointed by the Court of Protection, usually a family member. Apply on GOV.UK (search "become a deputy"), online or by post. You'll need it to run their bank accounts, pay care fees from savings and usually to sell their home.
  5. Get the capacity assessment
    Form COP3 is completed by their GP or another professional, confirming they can't make these decisions. The court may not accept the application without it. The GP may charge for this.
  6. Tell them, and 3 people who know them
    You must visit them and explain the application, and give them forms COP14PADep and COP5. Send form COP15PADep and COP5 to at least 3 people who know them, such as relatives or their social worker. They have 14 days to reply, and you must apply within 3 months of telling them.
  7. Pay the court fee
    The application fee is £432 (2026). Help with the fee depends on their finances: ask about form COP44A.
  8. Plan for the wait
    It often takes several months. If something is urgent, such as care fees that must be paid, ask the court about an urgent interim order. Tell the care home or council that the application is in progress.
  9. After you're appointed
    You may need to buy a security bond first. There's a one-off £100 assessment fee and a yearly supervision fee (£320, or £35 if they have under £21,000), and you send a report to the Office of the Public Guardian each year. Keep their money separate from yours and keep receipts.
  10. Health and care decisions
    These usually don't need a court application. Doctors, nurses and social workers decide in their best interests under the Mental Capacity Act and must consult family. Tell them you want to be involved. Health and welfare deputies are rarely appointed.
  11. If they move into a care home
    If they're under continuous supervision and not free to leave, the home must apply to the council for a Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards (DoLS) authorisation. You should be consulted, and you can ask questions or ask for a review. If there's no family, an independent advocate is appointed.
  12. Get free help
    Office of the Public Guardian 0300 456 0300. Court of Protection 0300 456 4600. Alzheimer's Society 0333 150 3456. Citizens Advice (CAB) Adviceline (England) 0800 144 8848. A solicitor who does Court of Protection work can apply for you, for a fee.
