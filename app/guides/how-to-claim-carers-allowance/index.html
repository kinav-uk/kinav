Claiming Carer's Allowance

£86.45 a week if you care 35 hours or more and earn £204 a week or less. Check first, as it can affect their benefits.

Step by step

  1. Check the rules
    You care for them at least 35 hours a week, and they get a qualifying benefit such as Attendance Allowance or the daily living part of PIP. You don't need to live with them or be related.
  2. Check your earnings
    After tax, National Insurance and half of any pension contributions, you must earn £204 a week or less (2026). Going even slightly over stops the whole payment for that week.
  3. Check before you claim
    If they get the severe disability extra in Pension Credit or other benefits, your Carer's Allowance can stop it. Ask Citizens Advice first: 0800 144 8848.
  4. Getting a State Pension?
    If your State Pension is more than £86.45 a week, Carer's Allowance won't be paid on top. Claiming can still add a carer amount to Pension Credit or Housing Benefit, so it's worth checking.
  5. Apply online
    Use GOV.UK (search "apply for Carer's Allowance"). Have their National Insurance number, your bank details and recent payslips ready. It can be backdated up to 3 months.
  6. Can't get it? Claim Carer's Credit
    If you care for 20 hours a week or more, Carer's Credit protects your State Pension, even if you can't get Carer's Allowance.
  7. Report changes straight away
    Tell the Carer's Allowance Unit if your earnings or hours change, or if they go into hospital or a care home, to avoid being overpaid.
  8. Get free help
    Carers UK 0808 808 7777. Citizens Advice Adviceline (England) 0800 144 8848.

Sources: GOV.UK: Carer's Allowance · Carers UK: help and advice. Last reviewed 24 October 2026.
