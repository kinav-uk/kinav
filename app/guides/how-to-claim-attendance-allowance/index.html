Claiming Attendance Allowance

Up to £114.60 a week if they're over State Pension age and need help. Savings and income don't matter.

Step by step

  1. Check they can claim
    They must be over State Pension age and have needed help or supervision for at least 6 months because of an illness or disability. It's about the help they need, not whether anyone gives it.
  2. Phone for the form
    Call the Attendance Allowance helpline on 0800 731 0122. If the form is returned within 6 weeks, payment is backdated to the day you called, so note the date. A downloaded form only counts from the day DWP receives it.
  3. Keep a diary first
    For a week, note each time they need help or someone keeping an eye on them, day and night, and how long it takes. Include falls, confusion and help at night.
  4. Answer for most days, including bad days
    Describe how things usually are, not their best day. Say what happens without help, such as a fall or a missed meal.
  5. Give examples
    "Needs help getting out of the bath, about 20 minutes each morning" says more than "struggles with washing".
  6. Know the two rates
    £76.70 a week for help in the day or at night. £114.60 a week for help both day and night, or if they're terminally ill.
  7. Add supporting evidence
    Include copies, not originals, of letters from the GP, hospital or occupational therapist if you have them, and name a professional who knows them.
  8. If they're terminally ill
    If they may not live more than 12 months, ask their doctor or nurse for form SR1. The claim is dealt with faster and paid at the higher rate.
  9. Sign, copy and send
    They sign it, or you can if you're their appointee or attorney. Keep a copy of everything you send.
  10. Check what else it unlocks
    An award can mean extra Pension Credit, a Council Tax reduction and Carer's Allowance for you. Use the benefits calculator.
  11. If it's refused
    Ask for a mandatory reconsideration within one month of the decision letter. Citizens Advice Adviceline (England) 0800 144 8848 can help.

Sources: GOV.UK: Attendance Allowance · Citizens Advice: Attendance Allowance. Last reviewed 24 October 2026.
