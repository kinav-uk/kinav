Applying for a Blue Badge

Parking closer to where they need to go. England's rules.

Tick off each step as you go in the free Kinav app.

Open this checklist

Step by step

  1. Check if they qualify automatically
    They do if they get the higher rate mobility part of DLA; PIP with 8 points or more for "moving around"; PIP with 10 points for descriptor E under "planning and following journeys"; are registered blind (severely sight impaired); or get War Pensioners' Mobility Supplement or certain Armed Forces compensation. Attendance Allowance on its own doesn't count.
  2. If not, they may still qualify
    The council decides. For example, if walking is very difficult because of pain, breathlessness or the time it takes, or isn't safe for them. Non-visible conditions can count too, such as being a serious risk near traffic, struggling severely to plan or follow a journey, or becoming extremely anxious in public places.
  3. Gather the documents
    A recent head-and-shoulders digital photo. A photo or scan of proof of identity (birth certificate, passport or driving licence) and proof of address (Council Tax bill or a recent government letter). Their National Insurance number, any benefit award letter (for PIP, the scores), and the current badge details if renewing.
  4. Get medical evidence, if they don't qualify automatically
    Check the council's own Blue Badge page first, as each council asks for different evidence. Many want letters from a consultant, specialist nurse, physiotherapist or occupational therapist, dated in the last 12 months. The letters should say how the condition affects walking or journeys, not just the diagnosis, how long it's likely to last, and what treatment has been tried. Letters about the outcome of an appointment count; appointment letters don't. Some councils say a GP letter alone isn't enough, and some professionals charge for letters.
  5. Getting their medical letters as their carer
    Hospital and clinic letters are usually copied to the GP. They can ask the GP surgery for copies of letters in their record, which is normally free. If they agree, the surgery can also give you proxy access in the NHS App so you can see their letters and record yourself; most surgeries ask you both to sign a consent form and show photo ID. If they can't make the decision themselves, a health and welfare Lasting Power of Attorney helps, and the GP decides what's appropriate.
  6. Write down how getting about affects them
    The form asks about their conditions and treatment, how far they can walk and how long it takes, what help they need, and the healthcare professionals involved in their care. Have their medicines list and the names of their GP, consultants and therapists to hand. A note of a typical trip, such as to the shops or the GP, makes this easier.
  7. Apply online
    Use GOV.UK: gov.uk/apply-blue-badge. You can apply on their behalf, and save and come back later. Or ask the council for a paper form.
  8. Pay the fee
    Up to £10 in England. The council tells you how to pay.
  9. Wait for the decision
    Usually within 12 weeks. The council may ask for more information, or invite them to a mobility assessment with an independent health professional. Keep copies of everything you send.
  10. If it's refused
    Ask the council to reconsider, explaining anything they missed. You can reapply if things change. Citizens Advice can help: Adviceline (England) 0800 144 8848.
  11. Use it the right way
    The badge is theirs: use it only when they're travelling in the car, as driver or passenger. Show it on the dashboard, with the clock where needed. Misuse can mean a fine of up to £1,000.
  12. Set a renewal reminder
    Badges usually last up to 3 years, and you must reapply before it runs out. In Calendar, choose Reminder, then Blue Badge renewal, and it will remind you 12 weeks before.

Sources: GOV.UK: apply for a Blue Badge. Last reviewed 24 October 2026.
