Living with arthritis

Practical help when joint pain and stiffness make everyday life harder.

Follow this plan and tick off each step in the free Kinav app.

Start the plan

Week 1: understand it and get support

  • Ask the GP which type of arthritis it is
    Osteoarthritis and inflammatory types, such as rheumatoid arthritis, are managed differently. Ask whether a referral to a specialist is needed.
  • Save the Versus Arthritis helpline
    0800 5200 520, free. Information and support for people with arthritis and their families.
  • Ask the pharmacist for a pain relief review
    Ask how and when to take their pain relief, and about anything that's making it hard to manage. The GP can review it too.
  • Ask who to contact if things get worse
    Ask the GP or specialist team what changes to tell them about, and who to call between appointments.

Weeks 2 to 3: movement and daily life

  • Ask for physiotherapy
    Ask the GP for a referral. In some areas you can refer yourself to NHS physiotherapy. Ask about local exercise programmes for joint pain.
  • Ask the council for a free OT home assessment
    An occupational therapist can suggest equipment such as grab rails, a perching stool, a raised toilet seat or kitchen aids. Equipment and small changes under £1,000 should be free in England.
  • Check feet and footwear
    Supportive shoes help. Ask the GP about NHS podiatry if feet are painful.
  • Apply for a Blue Badge if walking is hard
    See the Blue Badge kit.

Weeks 3 to 4: money, bigger changes and you

  • Claim Attendance Allowance or PIP
    Attendance Allowance if over State Pension age (usually once they've needed help for 6 months), Personal Independence Payment if under. Neither depends on savings. On the form, describe a typical bad day. Use the Benefits calculator.
  • Ask about a Disabled Facilities Grant
    Up to £30,000 in England for a stairlift, level-access shower or ramp. Means-tested. See the Home safety kit.
  • If a joint replacement is suggested
    Ask about waiting times and what they can do to prepare. In England they can usually choose which hospital they're referred to for their first appointment.
  • Ask the council for a needs assessment and a carer's assessment
    Both are free, whatever their savings.
  • Ask about day centres and respite
    Ask the social worker or council about day centres or day services: company, activities and a meal, often with transport, and a regular break for you. The council may help pay after a financial assessment. Ask about respite care too: short stays so you can rest or go away.
  • Check Carer's Allowance and Carer's Credit
    Carer's Allowance needs 35 hours a week of caring, earnings under the weekly limit, and the person you care for must get a qualifying benefit such as Attendance Allowance. Check first: it can stop the severe disability extra in their Pension Credit, so ask Citizens Advice. Carer's Credit, from 20 hours a week, protects your State Pension.
  • Ask the GP to record consent for you to discuss their care
    A signed letter from them is enough while they can decide. If they agree, ask the surgery about proxy access in the NHS App, so you can see letters and order repeat prescriptions.
  • Tell your employer
    One week of unpaid carer's leave a year, and the right to ask for flexible working, both from day one.

Sources: Versus Arthritis · Carers UK: help and advice. Last reviewed 24 October 2026.
