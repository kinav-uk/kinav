Just diagnosed: Parkinson's

Your first 30 days after a Parkinson's diagnosis, one step at a time.

Follow this plan and tick off each step in the free Kinav app.

Start the plan

Week 1: get support in place

  • Ask for a Parkinson's nurse
    Ask the neurologist or GP for a referral to a Parkinson's nurse specialist, your main contact between appointments.
  • Save the Parkinson's UK helpline
    0808 800 0303. Free advice from nurses and advisers.
  • Tell the DVLA and the car insurer
    Parkinson's must be reported to the DVLA. Not telling them risks a fine of up to £1,000.
  • Ask the Parkinson's nurse about medicine timings
    Ask what timings matter, and set phone reminders if that helps.

Weeks 2 to 3: therapy and money

  • Ask for therapy referrals
    Physiotherapy for movement and balance, occupational therapy for daily tasks, and speech and language therapy for speech or swallowing.
  • Ask the physio about exercise
    Ask what's right for them, and about local Parkinson's groups.
  • Claim Attendance Allowance or PIP
    Attendance Allowance if over State Pension age (usually once they've needed help for 6 months), Personal Independence Payment if under. Neither depends on savings. On the form, describe a typical bad day. Use the Benefits calculator.
  • Make both Lasting Powers of Attorney
    Property and financial affairs, and health and welfare, while they can make the decision. If it's too late, see the No Lasting Power of Attorney kit.

Weeks 3 to 4: home, care and you

  • Ask the council for a free OT home assessment
    To reduce falls and make daily tasks easier. See the Home safety kit.
  • Plan for hospital stays
    Parkinson's medicines need to be taken on time. If they go into hospital, tell staff their exact medicine times and take a written list. Parkinson's UK has free resources to help.
  • Ask the council for a needs assessment and a carer's assessment
    Both are free, whatever their savings.
  • Ask about day centres and respite
    Ask the social worker or council about day centres or day services: company, activities and a meal, often with transport, and a regular break for you. The council may help pay after a financial assessment. Ask about respite care too: short stays so you can rest or go away.
  • Check Carer's Allowance and Carer's Credit
    Carer's Allowance needs 35 hours a week of caring, earnings under the weekly limit, and the person you care for must get a qualifying benefit such as Attendance Allowance. Check first: it can stop the severe disability extra in their Pension Credit, so ask Citizens Advice. Carer's Credit, from 20 hours a week, protects your State Pension.
  • Tell your employer
    One week of unpaid carer's leave a year, and the right to ask for flexible working, both from day one.
  • Ask the GP to record consent for you to discuss their care
    A signed letter from your parent is enough while they can decide. If they agree, ask the surgery about proxy access in the NHS App too, so you can see letters and order repeat prescriptions.

Sources: Parkinson's UK · Carers UK: help and advice. Last reviewed 24 October 2026.
