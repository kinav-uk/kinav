Bladder and bowel problems

Practical help with incontinence. It's common, and often treatable.

Follow this plan and tick off each step in the free Kinav app.

Start the plan

Week 1: see the GP

  • Book a GP appointment
    Incontinence often has a treatable cause, such as an infection, constipation or medicines.
  • Ask for a referral to the NHS continence service
    Specialist nurses assess the problem and suggest treatment.
  • Ask which changes need a quick call
    Ask the GP what changes you should tell them about the same day.

Weeks 2 to 3: products and routines

  • Ask about free continence products
    After an assessment, the NHS may supply pads in some areas.
  • Ask about routines that might help
    The continence nurse can suggest what's right for them.
  • Make the toilet easy to reach
    A night light, clear path, raised toilet seat or commode. An OT can help.
  • Get a Radar key
    Opens accessible public toilets across the UK. Disability charities and some councils sell them.

Weeks 3 to 4: dignity and you

  • Ask how to care for their skin
    Ask the nurse or pharmacist what to use, and about any soreness.
  • Talk about it kindly
    Use their words, keep it private, and focus on comfort, not blame.
  • Claim Attendance Allowance or PIP
    Attendance Allowance if over State Pension age, Personal Independence Payment if under. Use the Benefits calculator.
  • Ask the council for a needs assessment and a carer's assessment
    Both are free, whatever their savings.
  • Check Carer's Allowance and Carer's Credit
    Carer's Credit from 20 hours a week, Carer's Allowance from 35.

Sources: Bladder & Bowel UK · Carers UK: help and advice. Last reviewed 24 October 2026.
