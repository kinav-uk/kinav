Poor mobility and falls

Your first 30 days of help when moving around gets harder, one step at a time.

Follow this plan and tick off each step in the free Kinav app.

Start the plan

Week 1: safety first

  • Ask the GP for a falls check and a medicines review
    Some medicines raise the risk of falls. Ask about the local falls prevention service.
  • Ask the council for a free OT home assessment
    Equipment and small changes under £1,000 should be free in England.
  • Set up a fall alarm
    See the Fall alarms and home cameras kit.
  • Make the home safer today
    Remove loose rugs and clutter, add night lights, and keep a phone within reach.

Weeks 2 to 3: strength and money

  • Ask for a physiotherapy referral
    Strength and balance exercises reduce falls. Ask about walking aids too.
  • Book an eye test and a hearing check
    NHS eye tests are free for over-60s. Poor sight and hearing raise the risk of falls.
  • Claim Attendance Allowance or PIP
    Use the Benefits calculator.
  • Apply for a Blue Badge
    Parking close to where they need to go.

Weeks 3 to 4: bigger changes and you

  • Ask about a Disabled Facilities Grant
    Up to £30,000 in England for a stairlift, level-access shower or ramp. Means-tested.
  • Check feet and footwear
    Well-fitting shoes with a good grip help. Ask the GP about NHS podiatry.
  • Ask the council for a needs assessment and a carer's assessment
    Both are free, whatever their savings.
  • Make both Lasting Powers of Attorney
    Plan ahead while they can make the decision.
  • Tell your employer
    One week of unpaid carer's leave a year, and the right to ask for flexible working, both from day one.

Sources: NHS: falls · Carers UK: help and advice. Last reviewed 24 October 2026.
