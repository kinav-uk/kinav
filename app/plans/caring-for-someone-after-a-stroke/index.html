After a stroke

Your first 30 days after a stroke, one step at a time.

Follow this plan and tick off each step in the free Kinav app.

Start the plan

Week 1: in hospital and coming home

  • Ask the stroke team what to watch for
    Ask what signs mean you should call 999, and write it down. The NHS website explains stroke symptoms.
  • Save the Stroke Association helpline
    0303 3033 100. Advice for stroke survivors and families.
  • Ask who is planning discharge, and for the plan in writing
    See the Leaving hospital kit.
  • Ask about early supported discharge and rehab
    Many areas offer therapy at home straight after hospital: physio, OT and speech and language therapy.
  • Stop driving and check the DVLA rules
    They must not drive for a calendar month after a stroke, and must tell the DVLA if problems remain after that.

Weeks 2 to 3: recovery and money

  • Keep a list of their medicines
    Take it to every appointment. Ask the pharmacist or GP any questions about it.
  • Ask about a 6-month review
    Everyone should have a review about 6 months after a stroke. Ask the GP or stroke team.
  • Claim Attendance Allowance or PIP
    Attendance Allowance if over State Pension age (usually once they've needed help for 6 months), Personal Independence Payment if under. Neither depends on savings. On the form, describe a typical bad day. Use the Benefits calculator.
  • Ask the council for a free OT home assessment
    Equipment and small changes under £1,000 should be free in England.
  • Apply for a Blue Badge if walking is hard
    See the Blue Badge kit. They can use it as a passenger too.

Weeks 3 to 4: care and you

  • Ask the council for a needs assessment and a carer's assessment
    Both are free, whatever their savings.
  • Ask about day centres and respite
    Ask the social worker or council about day centres or day services: company, activities and a meal, often with transport, and a regular break for you. Many only take people referred by a social worker, so ask them directly; others let you book and pay privately. The council may help pay after a financial assessment. Ask about respite care too: short stays so you can rest or go away.
  • Make both Lasting Powers of Attorney
    Property and financial affairs, and health and welfare, while they can make the decision. If it's too late, see the No Lasting Power of Attorney kit.
  • Check Carer's Allowance and Carer's Credit
    Carer's Allowance needs 35 hours a week of caring, earnings under the weekly limit, and the person you care for must get a qualifying benefit such as Attendance Allowance. Check first: it can stop the severe disability extra in their Pension Credit, so ask Citizens Advice. Carer's Credit, from 20 hours a week, protects your State Pension.
  • Tell your employer
    One week of unpaid carer's leave a year, and the right to ask for flexible working, both from day one.
  • Ask the GP to record consent for you to discuss their care
    A signed letter from them is enough while they can decide. If they agree, ask the surgery about proxy access in the NHS App, so you can see letters and order repeat prescriptions.

Sources: Stroke Association · Carers UK: help and advice. Last reviewed 24 October 2026.
