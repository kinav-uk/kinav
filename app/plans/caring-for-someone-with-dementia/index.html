Just diagnosed: dementia

Your first 30 days after a dementia diagnosis, one step at a time.

Follow this plan and tick off each step in the free Kinav app.

Start the plan

Week 1: get support in place

  • Get the diagnosis in writing and a named contact
    From the GP or memory clinic.
  • Save the helpline numbers
    Admiral Nurses 0800 888 6678 · Alzheimer's Society 0333 150 3456
  • Tell the DVLA and the car insurer
    Telling the DVLA is a legal requirement. Not telling them risks a fine of up to £1,000.
  • Ask the council for a free OT home assessment
    An occupational therapist checks the home before any fall or crisis. See the Home safety kit.
    Home safety and grants ›

Weeks 2 to 3: legal and money

  • Make both Lasting Powers of Attorney
    Property and financial affairs, and health and welfare. Do it while they can still decide. If it's too late, see the No Lasting Power of Attorney kit.
    No Lasting Power of Attorney? › · How to make an LPA ›
  • Check their will is up to date
  • Claim Attendance Allowance
    Not means-tested. Use the Benefits calculator to see what else may apply.
    How to claim Attendance Allowance › · Benefits calculator ›
  • Ask the council about a council tax discount
    Up to 100% off for severe mental impairment. It needs a GP certificate, and they must also get a qualifying benefit such as Attendance Allowance, so claim that first.
    How to claim Attendance Allowance › · Benefits calculator ›

Weeks 3 to 4: care and you

  • Ask the council for a needs assessment and a carer's assessment
    Both are free, whatever their savings.
    Letter asking for a needs assessment › · Carer's assessment letter › · Preparing for a needs assessment ›
  • Ask about day centres and respite
    Ask the social worker or council about day centres or day services: company, activities and a meal, often with transport, and a regular break for you. Many only take people referred by a social worker, so ask them directly; others let you book and pay privately. The council may help pay after a financial assessment. Ask about respite care too: short stays so you can rest or go away. Local dementia cafés and memory groups are often free: ask Alzheimer's Society or Age UK.
    Day centres: how they help ›
  • Check Carer's Allowance and Carer's Credit
    Carer's Allowance needs 35 hours a week of caring, earnings under the weekly limit, and the person you care for must get a qualifying benefit such as Attendance Allowance. Check first: it can stop the severe disability extra in their Pension Credit, so ask Citizens Advice. Carer's Credit, from 20 hours a week, protects your State Pension.
    How to claim Attendance Allowance › · How to claim Carer's Allowance › · Benefits calculator ›
  • Book a free fire safety visit
    From your local fire and rescue service.
  • Tell your employer
    One week of unpaid carer's leave a year, and the right to ask for flexible working, both from day one.
  • Ask the GP to record consent for you to discuss their care
    A signed letter from your parent is enough in the early stages. If they agree, ask the surgery about proxy access in the NHS App too, so you can see letters and order repeat prescriptions.

Sources: Alzheimer's Society · Carers UK: help and advice. Last reviewed 24 October 2026.
