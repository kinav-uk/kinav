End of life care

Making the last months as comfortable as possible, and knowing what to do.

Follow this plan and tick off each step in the free Kinav app.

Start the plan

Week 1: support and comfort

  • Ask the GP about palliative care
    Ask for a referral to the local palliative care team or hospice. Hospices also support people at home.
  • Save the Marie Curie Support Line
    0800 090 2309. Practical and emotional support, for them and for you.
  • Ask the hospice about day services
    Many hospices run day services with company and therapies, which also give carers a break. Ask about respite and night sitting too.
  • Ask about NHS Continuing Healthcare fast track
    If health is declining quickly, the NHS can fund care urgently. The GP or nurse completes the form.
    NHS Continuing Healthcare guide ›
  • Ask about benefits under the special rules
    If they may have 12 months or less to live, benefits such as Attendance Allowance can be paid faster at the highest rate. A doctor or nurse completes form SR1.
    How to claim Attendance Allowance › · Benefits calculator ›

Weeks 2 to 3: their wishes

  • Talk about what matters to them
    Where they want to be cared for, who they want with them, and any religious or cultural wishes.
  • Ask about an advance care plan
    Ask the GP about a ReSPECT form or advance care plan, and whether they want a DNACPR decision recorded.
  • Check their will and Lasting Powers of Attorney
    A will, and both Lasting Powers of Attorney, if not already made. If it's too late, see the No Lasting Power of Attorney kit.
    No Lasting Power of Attorney? › · How to make an LPA ›
  • Ask the GP to record consent for you to discuss their care
    A signed letter from them is enough while they can decide. If they agree, ask the surgery about proxy access in the NHS App, so you can see letters and order repeat prescriptions.
  • Ask about equipment and night support
    Hospital bed, commode, and night sitters from the hospice or Marie Curie.

Weeks 3 to 4: preparing

  • Know who to call, day and night
    Keep the numbers for the GP, district nurse, out-of-hours service and hospice in one place.
  • Ask about anticipatory medicines
    Medicines kept at home in case symptoms such as pain or breathlessness appear suddenly.
  • Know what happens after a death
    If the death is expected at home, you don't need to call 999: call the GP, out-of-hours service or hospice nurse to confirm the death. A medical examiner then reviews the cause of death, and their office tells you when you can register. Register within 5 days of that in England, then use Tell Us Once to inform government services.
  • Look after yourself
    Cruse Bereavement Support: 0808 808 1677. You can ask for your own carer's assessment too.

Sources: Marie Curie · Carers UK: help and advice. Last reviewed 24 October 2026.
