Council tax discounts for older people: living alone, disability and dementia

Many older people pay more council tax than they need to, because discounts aren't given automatically: you have to ask the council. Here are the main ones to check for an older parent in England, and how to apply. The council decides who qualifies.

Living alone: 25% off

If your parent is the only adult (18 or over) living in their home, they can get 25% off the bill. It doesn't depend on their income or savings.

Some people aren't counted when the council works out how many adults live there. These include a live-in carer (who isn't their partner, or the parent of a child under 18 in the home) and someone with a severe mental impairment. So your parent may still get 25% off even if someone lives with them.

Dementia or another severe mental impairment: up to 100% off

Someone who has a severe mental impairment, such as dementia, can get a big discount. They need a certificate from a doctor, such as their GP, and to get certain benefits, such as Attendance Allowance. The council has the full list of benefits.

  • 100% off if they live alone, or everyone else in the home also qualifies or is a full-time student.
  • 50% off if everyone else in the home isn't counted for council tax.
  • 25% off if they live with one other adult who is counted, such as a husband or wife.

A home with extra space for a disability: a lower band

The Disabled Band Reduction Scheme is for homes that are bigger than they'd need to be if no one there were disabled. The home needs one of these for the disabled person:

  • An extra bathroom, kitchen or other room they need.
  • Extra space inside to use a wheelchair.

How much the band reduction is

The bill is charged at the next band down, so a Band D home pays the Band C rate. A Band A home gets 17% off instead. The disabled person can be anyone whose main home it is: it doesn't have to be the person who pays the bill.

On a low income: Council Tax Reduction

If your parent has a low income and not much in savings, the council may reduce the bill through Council Tax Reduction (sometimes called Council Tax Support). People who get the Guarantee Credit part of Pension Credit can often get the whole bill covered. Many people who could get Pension Credit don't claim it, so it's worth checking.

Moved into a care home or hospital

If your parent has moved into a care home or hospital and their home is left empty, there's usually no council tax to pay on it, for as long as it stays empty. Tell the council the date they moved.

How to apply

  • Contact your parent's council: there's usually a form on its website. GOV.UK can find the council from a postcode.
  • Have their council tax account number ready (it's on the bill).
  • For a severe mental impairment, ask the GP for the certificate. The council may have its own form for the GP to sign.
  • Ask the council to backdate the discount to when your parent first qualified. Many councils will.
  • If they say no and you think they're wrong, you can ask them to look again, and then appeal.

Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland

Wales and Scotland have very similar discounts, but check with the local council. Northern Ireland has rates instead of council tax: see nidirect.gov.uk.

Check which other benefits your parent might be missing, such as Attendance Allowance and Pension Credit. It takes a few minutes.
