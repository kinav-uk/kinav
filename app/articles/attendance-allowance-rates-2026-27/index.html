Attendance Allowance rates 2026 to 2027: how much it is and who can get it

Attendance Allowance is £76.70 or £114.60 a week, depending on how much help your parent needs. It doesn't depend on savings or income, and many older people who could get it never claim. Here's what each rate is for, and what else it can unlock.

The two rates

  • Lower rate, £76.70 a week: for frequent help or constant supervision during the day, or supervision at night.
  • Higher rate, £114.60 a week: for help or supervision during the day and the night, or for someone a medical professional says is nearing the end of life.

Who can get it

  • People over State Pension age who need help with personal care, such as washing, dressing, eating or going to the toilet, or need someone to keep an eye on them.
  • They usually need to have needed the help for at least 6 months. This doesn't apply near the end of life.
  • It's about the help they need, not the help they get. They can still claim if they live alone or nobody helps them yet.
  • It doesn't depend on savings or income, and it isn't taxed.

What it can unlock

  • Extra Pension Credit, or Pension Credit for the first time.
  • Help with council tax, such as the discount for a severe mental impairment like dementia.
  • Carer's Allowance for someone who cares for them for 35 hours a week or more.

How it's paid

It's usually paid every 4 weeks into their bank account. It can be backdated only to the date the claim form was requested, so it's worth asking for the form as soon as you think they qualify.

How to claim

Claim on GOV.UK or ask for a paper form by phone. On the form, describe a bad day and how long things take, and include any help needed at night. Kinav's free step-by-step guide takes you through it.
