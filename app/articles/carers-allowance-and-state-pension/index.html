Carer's Allowance and State Pension: can you get both?

Many people caring for a parent are over State Pension age themselves. Carer's Allowance has no upper age limit, but it doesn't usually get paid on top of a State Pension. It can still be worth claiming. Here's why.

Carer's Allowance in 2026 to 2027

Carer's Allowance is £86.45 a week if you care for someone for at least 35 hours a week and they get a qualifying benefit, such as Attendance Allowance. There's an earnings limit: check GOV.UK for the current figure.

If you get a State Pension

The two are "overlapping benefits": you can't be paid both in full. If your State Pension is more than Carer's Allowance, you won't get Carer's Allowance paid. If it's less, you'll get the difference.

Why it can still be worth claiming

Even when it isn't paid, you can have what's called an "underlying entitlement" to Carer's Allowance. That can mean:

  • More Pension Credit: an extra amount for carers.
  • More help with rent (Housing Benefit) or council tax (Council Tax Reduction).

Check first: it can affect the person you care for

If your parent gets the extra amount for severe disability with their Pension Credit, it usually stops when someone gets Carer's Allowance for caring for them, and their council tax help can change. Overall, payments usually go up or stay the same, but it's worth getting a free benefits check before you claim.

Free help to check

  • Carers UK Helpline: 0808 808 7777.
  • Age UK Advice Line: 0800 678 1602.
  • Citizens Advice: 0800 144 8848 (England).
