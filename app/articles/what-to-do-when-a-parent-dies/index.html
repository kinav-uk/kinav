What to do when a parent dies: a practical checklist

When a parent dies there's a lot to do, often while you're grieving. This checklist covers the practical steps in England and Wales, in the order most families need them. You don't have to do everything at once, and you can ask for help.

In the first few days

  • The doctor or medical examiner will deal with the medical certificate of the cause of death. The registrar will tell you when you can register.
  • Register the death within 5 days, at a register office. In Scotland it's 8 days.
  • Ask the registrar about Tell Us Once: one service to tell most government departments, such as the DWP and HMRC.
  • Contact a funeral director when you're ready. Look for the will first, in case it says what your parent wanted.

Who else to tell

  • Their bank, building society and any pension providers.
  • The council: for council tax, and to stop any care they arranged or paid for.
  • The care home or care agency, and anyone else they paid regularly.
  • Energy, water, phone and TV suppliers, and their landlord if they rented.
  • Return any equipment, such as a hospital bed or a Blue Badge.

Money and the estate

  • Find out who the executor is: it's usually named in the will.
  • Value their money, property and possessions.
  • Probate may be needed before you can deal with their money or sell their home.
  • Benefits such as Attendance Allowance stop, and any overpayment may need to be paid back from the estate.

Help with funeral costs

If you're on certain benefits, you may be able to get a Funeral Expenses Payment from the government. Ask the funeral director for a full breakdown of costs before you agree.

Looking after yourself

Grief can be hard, especially after years of caring. Cruse Bereavement Support has a free helpline on 0808 808 1677. If you were a carer, Carers UK can help with what happens to your own benefits now.
