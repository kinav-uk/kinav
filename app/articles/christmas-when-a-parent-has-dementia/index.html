Christmas when a parent has dementia: practical tips for families

Christmas can be lovely, but busy rooms, noise and changes to routine can be hard for someone living with dementia. A little planning helps them, and you, enjoy it more.

Plan ahead

  • Keep the usual routine where you can, such as mealtimes, naps and getting up.
  • Think about the best time of day for them, and plan the main event then.
  • Keep gatherings smaller, or spread visitors over a few days.
  • Brief visitors: speak calmly, say who they are, and don't quiz them with “Do you remember…?”.
  • Have a plan B, in case they get tired or upset and need to leave early or skip something.

Decorations

  • Put decorations up gradually, over a few days, rather than all at once.
  • Familiar, traditional decorations are often more comforting than new ones.
  • Avoid flashing lights, and keep walkways clear to help prevent trips.

On the day

  • Have a quiet room they can go to for a break from noise and people.
  • Involve them in simple jobs they enjoy, such as laying the table, stirring, folding napkins or hanging decorations.
  • Familiar carols, old Christmas songs and photos often spark memories and conversation.
  • Keep the meal simple. Smaller portions on a plain plate, and a cup in a contrasting colour, can be easier.
  • Name cards on the table help them, and everyone else, with names.

If they live in a care home

  • Ask the home what's planned, and when visits work best.
  • A shorter visit at a quieter time is often better than a long one in a busy lounge.
  • If you can't visit, a phone or video call, a card or a small parcel still means a lot. Ask staff to help.

Look after yourself

Christmas can be tiring for carers, and it can bring up feelings about how things have changed. Share the jobs with family, and plan a break for yourself.
