Council tax and dementia: the severe mental impairment discount

If your parent has dementia, they may be able to pay less council tax, or none at all. It's called the severe mental impairment discount, and many families never hear about it. Here's who can get it in England and how to apply. The council decides who qualifies.

Who can get it

Your parent needs both:

  • A certificate from a doctor, such as their GP, saying they have a severe mental impairment. Dementia is a common reason.
  • To get certain benefits, such as Attendance Allowance or the daily living part of PIP. The council has the full list.

How much it's worth

  • 100% off: they live alone, or everyone else in the home also qualifies or is a full-time student.
  • 50% off: everyone else in the home isn't counted for council tax, such as a live-in carer.
  • 25% off: they live with one other adult who is counted, such as a husband or wife.

If they don't get Attendance Allowance yet

Attendance Allowance doesn't depend on savings or income, and isn't only for people who live alone. If your parent needs help with personal care or supervision because of dementia, it's worth claiming. It can also unlock this discount and more Pension Credit.

How to apply

  • Contact your parent's council: search their website for "severe mental impairment" or "council tax discount".
  • Ask the GP for the certificate. Many councils have their own form for the GP to sign. Some GP surgeries charge for this.
  • Send the council the form, their council tax account number and proof of the benefit, such as an award letter.
  • Ask for it to be backdated to when they first qualified. Many councils will backdate it, sometimes by years.

If they move into a care home

If your parent moves into a care home permanently and their home is left empty, there's usually no council tax to pay on it while it stays empty. Tell the council the date they moved.

Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland

Wales and Scotland have a similar discount: check with the local council. Northern Ireland has rates instead of council tax: see nidirect.gov.uk.
