Keeping an older parent warm and safe this winter: a checklist

Cold weather is hard on older people, especially those living alone. Most of what helps is simple and practical. Here's a checklist to go through on your next visit, or over the phone.

Warm home

  • The NHS advises heating the rooms they use most to at least 18°C, especially if they have a health condition.
  • Keep bedroom windows closed at night.
  • Check the heating, and the timer, work before the cold sets in. Get boilers and gas appliances checked by a Gas Safe registered engineer.
  • Make sure there's a working carbon monoxide alarm and smoke alarms.
  • Ask the local fire and rescue service about a free home fire safety visit.
  • Keep a torch somewhere easy to find, in case of a power cut. The free number for power cuts is 105.

Food, medicines and supplies

  • Keep a few days of food in, including things that don't need cooking, in case they can't get out.
  • Order repeat prescriptions early, especially before Christmas and bank holidays.
  • Check they have enough of anything they rely on, such as continence products.

Staying well

Kinav doesn't give health advice, but the NHS offers free winter vaccines to many older people. A free flu vaccine is offered to everyone aged 65 and over, and a COVID-19 vaccine to those aged 75 and over. Ask their GP surgery or a pharmacy.

If they start to feel unwell, the NHS says to get help early, from a pharmacy, their GP or NHS 111.

Out and about

  • Shoes or boots with good grip help on icy days.
  • Grit or salt for the front path and steps. Some councils provide grit bins.
  • If they don't need to go out on icy days, help them plan around it: a food delivery, or someone collecting prescriptions.

Keeping in touch

  • Agree who will call or pop in, and when, so someone checks in every day in very cold weather. Kinav's free Share out the jobs tool helps.
  • Ask a neighbour to keep an eye out, and swap numbers.
  • Write key numbers by the phone: family, GP, NHS 111, their energy supplier and 105 for power cuts.

Free help

  • Ask their energy and water companies to add them to the free Priority Services Register.
  • Check they're getting the winter payments they're due, such as the Winter Fuel Payment.
  • Age UK Advice Line: 0800 678 1602, free, every day.
  • Worried about an older person? You can also contact their council's adult social care team.
