Caring for someone with COPD this winter: how to cope

Winter can be the hardest time for families living with COPD. This article is about you, the carer: how to stay organised, share the load and look after yourself. For anything about their health, speak to their GP, respiratory nurse, pharmacist or NHS 111. World COPD Day is on 18 November.

Feeling anxious is normal

Living with someone's breathlessness can leave you on alert all the time, listening out at night and worrying when you're apart. That is very common, and it isn't a sign you're coping badly.

Naming the worry helps. Many carers find it easier once they know who to call and what the plan is.

Know who to call

  • Ask their GP or respiratory nurse whether they have a written plan for bad days, and ask for a copy for you.
  • Ask the professionals to explain, in their words, when to call them, when to call NHS 111 and when to call 999. Write it down.
  • Keep the GP, respiratory team, NHS 111 and pharmacy numbers by the phone and in your own phone.
  • Make sure family members have the same list.

Questions to take to the next appointment

  • What should we expect this winter?
  • What changes should we tell you about, and how quickly?
  • Is there anything I can learn to help, and who could teach me?
  • Are they eligible for pulmonary rehabilitation, and can I come too?
  • Who do we contact out of hours?

Share the load

  • Set up a shared family calendar for appointments, so you're not the only one keeping track. See the Appointments screen.
  • Agree a simple rota with family or friends for visits, shopping and lifts.
  • Ask the council for a needs assessment for them, and a carer's assessment for you. Both are free.
  • Ask about respite, so you can have regular breaks.

Look after yourself

Keep up your own appointments, sleep and time away. Ask your GP whether you can get a free flu vaccine as a carer.

Talk to someone. The Asthma + Lung UK helpline is 0300 222 5800 for questions about living with lung conditions. Samaritans are on 116 123, any time, if it all feels too much.
