Common questions

Will Mum have to sell her house to pay for care at home?

No. In England, the home is never counted when someone gets care in their own home.

Is the house counted if they move into a care home?

It can be, but not if their partner, a relative aged 60 or over, a relative who is disabled, or a child under 18 they support still lives there.

Does the house have to be sold straight away?

No. If the home is counted, it's ignored for the first 12 weeks of a permanent stay, and a deferred payment agreement with the council means it doesn't have to be sold in their lifetime.

When does the council start to help pay?

When savings and other assets fall below £23,250. Below £14,250, they're ignored.

Can we give the house away to avoid care fees?

Giving the home away to avoid care fees can be treated as if they still own it.

Good to know

  • If the NHS agrees they qualify for NHS continuing healthcare, the NHS pays for their care.
  • The council's financial assessment decides, so ask for the result in writing.
  • Free help: Citizens Advice 0800 144 8848 and Age UK 0800 678 1602. For regulated financial advice, the Society of Later Life Advisers.
